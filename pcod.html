Understanding PCOD — and living well with it

A clear, honest guide to Polycystic Ovarian Disease — what it is, how it affects your body, and what you can do every day to feel better.

What is PCOD

A hormonal condition, not a life sentence

Polycystic Ovarian Disease (PCOD) — also called PCOS — is a hormonal disorder affecting 1 in 10 women of reproductive age. The ovaries produce more androgens (male hormones) than usual, which can disrupt ovulation and cause small cysts to form. It is manageable, and many women live full, healthy lives with the right information and habits.

🧬
Hormonal imbalance
Elevated androgens (like testosterone) interfere with the monthly release of eggs. This causes irregular or absent periods and affects fertility.
🫧
Ovarian cysts
Small fluid-filled follicles develop on the ovaries. Despite the name, these are not true cysts — they are immature egg follicles that didn't fully develop.
📊
Metabolic effects
PCOD is closely linked to insulin resistance, which affects how your body processes blood sugar. This is why weight management and diet have such a strong impact.
🌱
Highly manageable
Lifestyle changes — especially diet, exercise, and sleep — can dramatically reduce symptoms. Many women see improvement within weeks of consistent changes.
Symptoms

How PCOD shows up

Symptoms vary widely between individuals. You may have all of these, some of them, or just one. The presence of 2 of the 3 main criteria (irregular periods, high androgens, polycystic ovaries on scan) is typically enough for a diagnosis.

Insulin Resistance

The insulin connection — why it matters

Up to 70% of women with PCOD have insulin resistance. Understanding this mechanism is key to understanding why diet and exercise have such a powerful impact on PCOD management.

1

You eat carbohydrates

Blood glucose rises. Your pancreas releases insulin to move glucose from blood into cells for energy.

2

Cells stop responding properly

In insulin resistance, cells become "deaf" to insulin's signal. The pancreas compensates by producing even more insulin.

3

High insulin triggers more androgens

Excess insulin stimulates the ovaries to produce more testosterone. This worsens PCOD symptoms — more acne, more hair growth, more irregular cycles.

4

Fat storage increases

High insulin is a fat-storage hormone. This makes weight loss harder, particularly around the abdomen — even when eating less.

5

The cycle continues

More weight, more insulin resistance, more androgens, worse symptoms. Breaking this cycle through diet and exercise is the most effective intervention available.

💡 The good news: Insulin resistance responds quickly to lifestyle changes. Even a 5–10% reduction in body weight can significantly improve insulin sensitivity, restore ovulation, and reduce androgen levels. You don't need to reach an "ideal" weight — small consistent changes create real hormonal change.

Do's & Don'ts

What actually helps

These aren't rigid rules — they are evidence-based guidelines that reduce insulin resistance, balance hormones, and ease symptoms. Small, consistent choices matter more than dramatic changes.

✅ Do
Eat low-glycemic foods — brown rice, oats, lentils, legumes, most vegetables
Prioritise protein at every meal — eggs, fish, chicken, paneer, dal
Do strength training 2–3 times a week — builds muscle, improves insulin sensitivity
Walk after meals — even 10–15 minutes lowers blood glucose spikes
Sleep 7–9 hours — poor sleep raises cortisol and worsens insulin resistance
Manage stress actively — yoga, breathing, journalling, therapy
Eat fiber-rich foods — slows glucose absorption, feeds gut bacteria
Stay hydrated — 2–2.5L water daily
Consider inositol (Myo + D-Chiro) — improves insulin sensitivity in PCOD
Track your cycle, symptoms and food — patterns reveal your personal triggers
🚫 Don't
Skip meals — causes blood sugar crashes and increases cortisol
Eat refined carbs alone — white bread, white rice, naan, luchi without protein
Drink sugary drinks — soft drinks, packaged juices, sweetened lassi
Over-exercise with intense cardio only — can increase cortisol and worsen symptoms
Eat late at night — disrupts blood sugar and sleep quality
Ignore stress — chronic stress worsens every PCOD symptom
Crash diet — very low calorie diets spike cortisol and worsen hormonal imbalance
Consume excess dairy if it triggers symptoms — some women find it worsens acne
Self-medicate hormones — always work with a doctor for metformin, birth control, etc.
Compare your journey — PCOD presents differently for everyone
🍽️
The PCOD plate principle
At each meal: fill ½ your plate with non-starchy vegetables, ¼ with lean protein, and ¼ with complex carbs (not white rice alone). Always pair carbs with protein or fat to slow glucose absorption. Never eat carbs on an empty stomach.
