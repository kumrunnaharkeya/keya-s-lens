Insulin Resistance
& Type 2 Diabetes
Understanding how insulin works — and why it sometimes stops working — is the first step to reversing it.
🔬 What is insulin?
Insulin is a hormone made by the pancreas. Its job is simple: move glucose (sugar) from your blood into your cells so they can use it for energy.

Think of insulin as a key and your cells as locked doors. After you eat, blood sugar rises, the pancreas releases insulin, insulin unlocks the cells, and glucose flows in. Blood sugar drops. Everything works.

With insulin resistance (IR), the locks become stiff. The key still exists but the doors don't open properly. Your pancreas compensates by producing more and more insulin — until it eventually cannot keep up. That's when blood sugar stays elevated, and Type 2 Diabetes develops.
⚙️ How insulin resistance develops
1
Repeated glucose spikes
A diet high in refined carbs (white rice, bread, sugar) causes frequent large blood sugar spikes. Each spike demands a large insulin response.
2
Cells become desensitised
Constantly bombarded with insulin, cells start downregulating their insulin receptors — the same way you stop hearing background noise. The signal gets ignored.
3
Pancreas overworks
To compensate, the pancreas produces 3–5× more insulin than normal. This hyperinsulinaemia causes fat storage (especially belly fat), inflammation, and hormonal disruption.
4
Liver joins in
High insulin tells the liver to keep producing glucose even when blood sugar is already high — making the problem worse.
5
Pre-diabetes → Type 2 Diabetes
When the pancreas can no longer produce enough insulin to overcome resistance, fasting blood sugar stays high. Pre-diabetes (FBS 100–125 mg/dL) progresses to Type 2 Diabetes (FBS ≥126 mg/dL) if not reversed.
🧬 How it affects your body
Insulin resistance doesn't just affect blood sugar. Because insulin is involved in almost every metabolic process, its dysfunction causes a cascade of effects throughout the body.
🫀
Heart & vesselsHigh insulin damages blood vessel walls, raises triglycerides, and lowers HDL — the biggest risk factor for heart disease.
🧠
BrainThe brain is an insulin-sensitive organ. IR impairs memory, focus and mood. Some researchers call Alzheimer's "Type 3 Diabetes."
⚖️
Fat storageInsulin is a storage hormone. High insulin means your body is always in "store fat" mode — especially visceral (belly) fat.
🌸
Hormones (PCOD)High insulin stimulates the ovaries to produce excess testosterone — directly causing PCOD symptoms.
🦷
InflammationChronic high insulin triggers systemic inflammation — worsening joint pain, acne, gut health and autoimmune conditions.
🩸
Blood pressureInsulin causes sodium retention and arterial stiffness — a common but underrecognised cause of hypertension.
😴
Sleep & energyBlood sugar rollercoasters cause energy crashes, afternoon fatigue, and poor sleep quality — worsening IR further.
🍬
CravingsAfter a glucose spike, insulin overreacts and drops blood sugar too low — creating intense carb and sugar cravings within 90 minutes.
⚠️ Early warning signs of IR
Many people have insulin resistance for years before blood sugar rises. Watch for:

🔸 Dark, velvety patches on neck, armpits or groin (acanthosis nigricans)
🔸 Strong sugar or carb cravings 1–2 hours after eating
🔸 Afternoon energy crash (2–4 PM)
🔸 Belly fat that won't shift despite dieting
🔸 Waking up tired even after 8 hours sleep
🔸 Skin tags (small growths in skin folds)
🔸 Brain fog and difficulty concentrating
🔸 Frequent urination, especially at night

✅ The good news — it's reversible
Unlike Type 1 Diabetes, insulin resistance and Type 2 Diabetes are largely lifestyle-driven — and lifestyle-reversible.

Clinical studies show that:
🟢 A 5–10% reduction in body weight significantly improves insulin sensitivity
🟢 Post-meal walking (10 min) reduces glucose spikes by up to 30%
🟢 Strength training 2–3×/week improves insulin sensitivity for 24–48h
🟢 A low-GI diet with adequate fiber reduces fasting insulin within 4 weeks
🟢 7–9 hours sleep is as important as diet for IR reversal
🟢 Metformin + inositol (for PCOD) can help when lifestyle alone is insufficient

You don't need to be perfect. Consistent small changes compound over weeks and months.

📌 What drives T2D risk
🍬
High sugar & refined carbs
Daily sugar >30g and carb loads >250g consistently spike insulin and deplete pancreatic reserve.
😴
Poor sleep
Under 6 hours increases cortisol and growth hormone — both raise fasting blood sugar directly.
⚖️
BMI and waist size
Visceral fat around the waist produces inflammatory chemicals that block insulin receptors.
🌸
PCOD
Up to 70% of women with PCOD have insulin resistance — they have a 3–7× higher lifetime risk of T2D.
🧬
Genetics
South Asian populations (including Bangladeshis) develop T2D at a BMI 3–5 points lower than Western thresholds — and often at younger ages.
